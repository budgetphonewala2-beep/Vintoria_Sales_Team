## Who buys this (segments)
- **Funded founder before build** - buying a defensible direction and a scope before engineering spend. Trigger: two build quotes land far apart, or an investor asks for a roadmap.
- **Corporate digital lead in a mid-market firm** - buying an independent read on a stalled programme. Trigger: a board review date, a new CDO or CTO in seat, or a legacy platform contract expiring.
- **Non-technical founder with an idea only** - buying a feasibility answer and a plan they can fund. Trigger: an accelerator, grant or competition deadline.
- **PE or VC operating partner** - buying a fact-based view of a portfolio company's product and stack. Trigger: new investment, diligence window, or a turnaround mandate.
- **Product leader at a scaling company** - buying prioritisation that survives a planning cycle. Trigger: annual planning, a missed quarter, or a team reorganisation.
- **Dev shop or agency without a strategy arm** - buying discovery capacity before a signed build. Trigger: a client contract signed with no discovery phase scoped.
- **Post-launch founder with flat traction** - buying a diagnosis of why the product is not pulling. Trigger: two flat quarters or a churn review that ended without an answer.
- **Transformation programme owner** - buying process and system analysis before a platform replacement. Trigger: merger integration, or an ERP or CRM replacement decision.

## Buying signals (observable)
1. Careers page advertises a senior product role while no product documentation is referenced anywhere on the site (002, 006).
2. Site names three or more unrelated product lines with no shared positioning sentence (002, 013).
3. A "roadmap" or "coming soon" page exists and its last dated entry is over a year old (010).
4. A news or press page announces expansion into a market with no market-specific page on the site (008).
5. Two job ads describe the same function in different business units (005).
6. A press release states a launch date that has passed and no live product is linked (007, 009).
7. The founder posts publicly asking which stack, framework or platform to choose (003, 012).
8. The site's page source shows an ageing CMS build with visible plugin paths (012).
9. A pricing page exists but names no segment, no unit and no outcome (013, 015).
10. Blog or changelog shows a long gap followed by a burst of posts (002).
11. A job ad carries "digital transformation" in the title with no programme described (004, 011).
12. The product page lists features with no stated job, outcome or user (006, 014).
13. A published milestone carries no owner and no date on the site (010).
14. A waitlist form is live with no pricing, no segments and no stated launch date (007, 015).
15. A founder interview or podcast shownote describes scope creep or a rebuild (005, 007).

## Discovery questions
1. Which decision does this work need to unlock, and who signs it off?
2. What have you already built or bought, and what did it fail to answer?
3. Which two options are you choosing between this month?
4. Who inside the company disagrees with the current plan, and on what?
5. Which funding, board or budget event does the output feed into?
6. What would make you cancel this initiative outright?
7. Who owns the product after the strategy is delivered?
8. Which constraint is fixed: team, budget, technology or date?

## Proof-of-work audit angles
1. Count distinct product lines named on the site; if more than three, list them and check for a shared positioning line (002, 013).
2. Search the site and its archive for "roadmap", "coming soon", "beta"; record each hit with its date (010).
3. Read the last six news or blog posts and record the interval between them (002, 008).
4. List every open role on the careers page and mark each as product, engineering or transformation (005, 011).
5. Quote the pricing page verbatim and check whether it names a segment, a unit and an outcome (013, 015).
6. Quote the homepage headline and the about-page mission; report whether they say the same thing (002).
7. List announcements on the news page and mark any stated milestone the site does not show as delivered (007, 009).
8. Note the CMS or generator visible in page source, with the URL checked (012).
9. Count homepage calls to action and note whether they all lead to one action (014).
10. Locate the changelog and record its most recent dated entry (010).

- Board-ready strategy summary

## Common objections
| Objection | Real concern | Reframe | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| "We already have a strategy deck." | Paying twice for the same document. | The work tests the deck against what the site and team actually show. | The deck, plus audit findings that contradict it. |
| "We just need developers." | An unbounded build that eats the budget. | Unscoped builds are how budgets disappear; a short scope narrows the build. | The current requirement list and the count of unresolved decisions. |
| "Strategy takes too long." | Delay before revenue. | The first output can be a one-page decision memo, not a full plan. | The decision date already on the buyer's calendar. |
| "Our team can do this internally." | Cost and control. | Internal teams know the business; an outside read tests the assumptions they cannot see. | Whether that team shipped a product decision in the last two quarters. |
| "How do we know the recommendation is right?" | Risk of a wrong direction. | Recommendations arrive as evidence plus options, not a single answer. | The audit findings and the named assumptions. |

## Do not sell when
- The buyer wants a document produced to a template with no decision attached to it.
- No one with budget authority will attend discovery.
- The initiative has no named owner after delivery.
- The real request is staff augmentation billed by the day.
- The core question is legal, tax, regulatory or investment advice.
- A fixed specification is already written and the exercise is procurement.
- The buyer will not share site, analytics or product access for the audit.
