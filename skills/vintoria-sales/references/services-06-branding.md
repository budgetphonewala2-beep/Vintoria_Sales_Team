## Who buys this (segments)
- **Funded startups** - buying startup branding, identity and a deck that matches the raise. Trigger: a funding round is announced.
- **Companies after a merger or acquisition** - buying corporate branding and a merged visual identity. Trigger: the deal is publicly announced.
- **Product teams launching a new line** - buying product branding and a product-level brand system. Trigger: a new product or SKU is announced.
- **SaaS companies whose site predates their positioning** - buying brand positioning, voice and visual identity. Trigger: the category narrative shifts or a competitor repositions.
- **Multi-location and franchise businesses** - buying brand guidelines and asset kits for operators. Trigger: a new location, franchisee or reseller is onboarded.
- **Agencies needing overflow creative** - buying white-label marketing creative and presentation design. Trigger: a campaign deadline cannot be staffed.
- **Founders preparing for investment or exit** - buying presentation design and brand guidelines. Trigger: due diligence or an investor meeting is booked.
- **Marketplace sellers moving to their own brand** - buying social media brand kits and logo design. Trigger: platform dependency becomes a stated risk.

## Buying signals (observable)
- New logo or domain visible in the last twelve months on the site or LinkedIn (105, 115).
- Different logo colours or lockups across website header, social avatar and app icon (106, 107).
- Careers page or press page mentions a rebrand, new identity or new brand platform (115, 103).
- Press release announcing funding, a merger or a leadership change (103, 113, 114).
- Public pitch deck screenshots with inconsistent typography or layout between slides (111).
- Social profiles using mismatched avatars, banners and typefaces (110, 109).
- No brand or press kit page while the site offers partner, reseller or media resources (107).
- Site copy uses category language the current product no longer matches (116, 117).
- Multiple sub-brands or product names with no visible hierarchy rule (112, 106).
- Job ad for a brand manager, creative lead or head of brand (103, 118).
- App icon or packaging updated while the website still shows the previous mark (105, 119).
- Palette divergence measurable between site, deck and social captures (109).
- Linked PDFs or one-pagers using three different heading typefaces (108, 120).
- About page reads as company history rather than a stated position (116, 117).

## Discovery questions
- What changed in the business that made the current brand feel wrong?
- Who approves brand decisions now, and who approved them last time?
- Which assets are produced every week: deck, social, packaging, site, product?
- Is there a written position, or does it live only with the founders?
- Which sub-brands, products or locations must the system cover?
- What did the last brand engagement fail to deliver?
- Where do partners or resellers get your assets today?
- What would you refuse to change: name, logo, colour or none of these?

## Proof-of-work audit angles
- Collect the logo from site header, favicon, app icon and social avatar; report each file and whether the marks differ.
- Sample rendered heading and body fonts across five pages; report the family and weight set per page.
- Extract the palette from captures of site, deck and social; report hex values and where they diverge.
- Check for a public brand or press kit; report which assets it contains and which are missing.
- Read homepage hero, about page and meta description; report the position each one states.
- List every product or sub-brand name and the visible relationship rule; report the hierarchy as shown.
- Compare tone across site, help centre and social bios; report concrete phrase-level differences.
- Render the logo at favicon size and in single colour; report both outputs.
- Identify the most recent public identity change; report the date and the source.
- Check publicly linked PDFs and decks for template consistency; report which deviate.

## Do not sell when
- Leadership cannot agree who owns the brand decision.
- They want a logo only, refuse positioning work, and complain the brand is unclear.
- A trademark dispute on the name is unresolved.
- No recurring asset production exists, so guidelines would have no user.
- An acquisition is imminent and the acquirer will impose identity.
- The request is a one-off file conversion or vector trace.
- The brief names a competitor brand to copy exactly.
