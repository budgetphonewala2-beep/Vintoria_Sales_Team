## Who buys this (segments)

- **Local service business with several branches.** Buying local SEO, location pages and review handling. Trigger: a new branch opening or a drop in map visibility.
- **B2B software team with a content backlog.** Buying SEO, content marketing and keyword research. Trigger: a new demand-gen owner or a flat pipeline target.
- **DTC brand scaling paid media.** Buying paid advertising, retargeting and landing page optimisation. Trigger: rising acquisition cost or a new budget cycle.
- **Multi-location franchise.** Buying programmatic SEO, local SEO and marketing analytics at scale. Trigger: a franchisee complaint about local visibility.
- **Publisher or directory with a large URL set.** Buying technical SEO, programmatic SEO and search marketing. Trigger: an indexing or site migration issue.
- **Startup preparing a launch.** Buying digital marketing strategy, keyword research and landing page optimisation. Trigger: a launch date fixed by the board.
- **Agency needing white-label delivery.** Buying SEO, paid and content execution under their brand. Trigger: a signed retainer they cannot staff.
- **Company that ran ads without measurement.** Buying marketing analytics, conversion tracking and a funnel rebuild. Trigger: a finance review of unmeasured ad spend.

## Buying signals (observable)

1. Robots file blocks sections that carry revenue pages, or the sitemap is missing (213).
2. Several templates share the same title tag or have no meta description (216, 212).
3. Google Business Profile has unanswered reviews, missing hours or no posts (214).
4. Location or category pages look hand-built and near-duplicate at scale (215).
5. Blog or resource section shows no new post in the visible last twelve months (218, 227).
6. Ad library shows active ads whose landing page does not repeat the ad claim (222, 233).
7. No analytics or tag manager script is detectable in the page source (231).
8. Careers page lists an SEO, paid media or content role (212, 223, 227).
9. Brand plus category searches surface competitors above the brand (220, 212).
10. Backlink profile is dominated by generic directories and low-relevance sites (217).
11. Core Web Vitals fail when the public page is tested this session (213, 262).
12. Newsletter signup exists but no welcome or follow-up page is linked (228).
13. Old campaign pages remain indexed and now show outdated offers (215, 216).
14. FAQ, product or local pages carry no structured data in the page source (213, 216).

## Discovery questions

1. Which channel produced the last ten customers, and how do you know?
2. What did you try in the last year, and what did you stop?
3. Which pages or terms matter most to revenue, by name?
4. Who owns the site, the analytics and the ad accounts today?
5. What is the monthly budget for media, separate from the work?
6. Which market and language must be covered first?
7. What does a qualified lead look like once it reaches sales?
8. Is there a page or campaign you refuse to change?

## Proof-of-work audit angles

- Crawl the visible navigation and record missing titles, duplicate titles and missing meta descriptions by URL.
- Read the robots file and sitemap and list any revenue section that is blocked or absent.
- Test three important URLs for mobile rendering and record layout or tap-target failures observed.
- Search the brand name and two category terms, and record which pages rank above the brand.
- Read the Google Business Profile and count unanswered reviews and missing fields.
- Open the ad library and record whether each active ad has a matching landing page.
- Inspect page source for analytics, tag manager and structured data, and record what is absent.
- Check the newest and oldest posts in the blog and record the gap in months.
- Sample ten backlinks and record how many are directories or unrelated to the niche.
- Check canonical tags across filtered or paginated collection pages.

## Common objections

| Objection | Real concern | One-line reframe | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| We tried SEO and nothing happened | Wasted budget and no accountability | Start with an audit that stands on its own | Findings register with one URL per item |
| Paid ads are cheaper than SEO | Time to result | Both are measured against the same funnel | Measurement plan separating paid and organic outcomes |
| We have an in-house marketer | Overlap and ownership | Define the handover boundary, work only the named gaps | Scope list that excludes the in-house remit |
| How long until page one | Certainty of return | No ranking or timing promise, work is judged on delivered fixes | Reporting definitions agreed before work starts |
| Our agency already sends reports | Duplicated reporting | Audit their reports for definitions and missing sources | Review of the current report against the measurement plan |

## Do not sell when

- The buyer will not share site, analytics or ad account access in any form.
- The goal is a ranking, traffic or revenue guarantee.
- The budget covers media but not the work, or the reverse, with no room to split.
- The site is due to be replaced within the planning window.
- The market or language is outside the team's verifiable capability.
- The ask is purchased email lists or automated bulk social messaging.
- Only a single one-off post or ad edit is wanted.
